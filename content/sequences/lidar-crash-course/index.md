A short textbook on LiDAR, meant to be read in order. It begins with the physics and electronics of the sensor and the structure of the data it produces, and works through the algorithms an engineer working with LiDAR is expected to know: the geometry of coordinate frames and rigid transforms, spatial data structures and neighbour search, normals and local features, robust model fitting and ground segmentation, clustering, scan registration, odometry and SLAM, occupancy mapping, multi-object tracking, and the deep networks that detect objects in point clouds. Each writeup builds on the ones before it, and together they are the background assumed by the writeups of research papers elsewhere on the site.
